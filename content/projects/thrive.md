- summary: Funded by North Valley Thrive, this one-year project assessed the viability of bringing solar lighting products, and ultimately clean-energy manufacturing, to the North San Joaquin Valley. Working with industry partner Portor Lighting, a faculty-led team of three student research assistants confirmed market and community interest and identified lighting needs across campuses, residential communities, and public spaces. The team also used AI to build market intelligence, studied the U.S. supply chain and the intellectual property landscape, confirmed a pathway to Buy America (BABA 2021) compliance, and developed a training framework for local technicians. The project is now ready for its next stage.

## Student learning
- intro: The project was designed as experiential learning. Students did the work of a startup team, with real stakes, real partners, and real deadlines.
- Job readiness | Students owned research, data analysis, and reporting on a biweekly cycle, and two of the research assistants received full-time job offers by the end of the project.
- Industry mentorship | Biweekly reviews with Portor Lighting and a visit to its facility in Ontario, CA put students alongside industry veterans in product, manufacturing, and sales.
- AI in daily work | AI was part of the workflow, not a side topic. The team used AI tools such as Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, HeyGen, and Newport AI to build domain knowledge, brainstorm ideas, prepare and analyze data, write code, generate leads, craft stories for different audiences, and produce outreach videos with digital avatars.
- Outreach and communication | Students presented at school events, joined an investor pitch, and met with campus leaders, local businesses, and regional organizations, using AI to shape the storytelling and outreach materials.
- Building for the next stage | Students developed a technician training framework, a bill of materials, and a U.S. supplier list that prepare the project for its next phase.

## Student work
### Field test of the solar bollard
- text: Pilot installations at residential sites in Northern California and on-campus demonstrations tested how the solar bollard performs at night, including its battery and electrical output.
- image: projects/thrive/field-test.jpg

### LightScope campus siting map
- text: A night-time field survey of University of the Pacific sites became an interactive siting map: 13 lighting zones, 28 assessed points, and 64 planned fixture locations.

### AI lead-intelligence platform
- text: AI agents drew on six public data sources to find and rank places that could benefit from solar lighting: 300+ potential sites identified and 100+ opportunities ranked across 3 major market segments.

### Live market dashboard
- text: With AI-assisted data preparation and coding, students built an e-commerce and residential dashboard in SQL, Python, and Google Looker Studio, combining market research and competitor analysis to support decisions.

### Bill of materials and U.S. suppliers
- text: Students built a bill of materials (BOM) for a commercial solar bollard and identified U.S. vendors for its components, paving the way for domestic, BABA-compliant manufacturing in the next stage.

### Market, supplier, and IP landscape
- text: AI tools helped the team build domain knowledge quickly. The result was a competitive analysis of the solar lighting market, supported by legal consulting to understand the intellectual property landscape of the U.S. solar lighting industry.

### Pitch, video, and community outreach
- text: An investor pitch at Smart Living Silicon Valley 2026, a pilot proposal for Stockton's Miracle Mile, and an "Invest in Stockton" outreach video on clean-energy manufacturing, presented by an AI digital avatar.
- link: https://app.heygen.com/videos/invest-in-stockton-the-future-of-clean-energy-manufacturing-f579956b64304a1eb71ac71f01abcdc3

### Workforce training framework
- text: A training framework for Solar Light Product Assembly Technicians, with a plan to grow it into a high school summer camp, plus an AI-assisted marketing lecture for local workforce development.

## Preparing for a classroom pilot
- intro: The next step brings THRIVE into the classroom. We are preparing a first classroom pilot of "Take It to Market," a course model in which students learn AI by doing it: AI is built into every step of a real business workflow, and what three student researchers did in one funded year, a whole class can do.
- photo: projects/thrive/take-it-to-market-poster.jpg
- caption: "Take It to Market" poster, University of the Pacific AI Showcase & Symposium, November 12, 2026.
- Learn the industry: AI finds and summarizes research, and students write a background report.
- Analyze the market: AI writes code to collect and clean data, and students build a dataset and dashboard.
- Understand customers: AI pre-tests surveys and codes answers, and students build segment profiles.
- Build a lead agent: AI agents find and rank prospects, and students deliver a ranked lead list.
- Pitch and take action: AI helps build the website, video, and pitch, and students run outreach and pilots.

## Long-term vision
- intro: With Phase II grant funding or venture investment, the project could grow in three steps:
- 1–1.5 years | A new local entity, a pilot manufacturing facility, and a BABA-compliant prototype | 3–4 full-time jobs
- 3 years | Manufacturing, sales, and service operations reach break-even | 10–15 full-time jobs
- 5 years | A fully operational manufacturer with protected IP and in-house design, manufacturing, and installation | 40–50 full-time jobs
